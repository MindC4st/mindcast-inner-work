# 11 · Video Sourcing Sheet — All 52 Weeks × 3 Tracks

*Every week, every track, with its theme — and a blank for you to drop a YouTube
URL against. **Child track first**, since it needs the most work and matters most.*

**Spreadsheet version:** `exports/VIDEO-SOURCING-SHEET.csv` — open it in Excel or
Sheets, fill the **NEW VIDEO URL** column, and send it back. I'll turn it into a
migration and the app will pick it up.

**Legend**
- 🔴 **REPLACE** — this week currently shares its video with another week. Needs its own.
- ⚪ **keep** — already unique, or it's the one week that keeps the shared video.
  Only change it if you want something better.

You can of course replace a ⚪ **keep** too — the sheet just marks the minimum
needed to remove all reuse.

**Before anything goes live**, run `node scripts/verify-video-urls.mjs` on a machine
that can reach YouTube. It confirms each video exists, prints its **real title and
channel** (so a wrong-but-valid ID gets caught), and flags any remaining reuse.

### What to check before you paste a URL

1. **Is it the original source?** The creator's own channel, TED, TED-Ed, RSA —
   not a re-upload. A re-upload can vanish overnight and it's the thing the
   [IP review](legal/05_ip_review_52_weeks.md) flagged.
2. **Does embedding work?** Some videos are watch-on-YouTube-only. Open the
   URL, click Share → Embed. If there's no embed option, it will show a blank
   box on the big screen.
3. **Child track — watch it end to end.** Including the last thirty seconds and
   whatever the channel autoplays into. This is the one place a mistake is a
   safeguarding problem rather than a content problem.
4. **Does it serve *this* week's theme,** not just the general topic? The video
   sits after "In Today's World" and its job is evidence: proof, a personal
   story, or a how-to for the thing just taught.

### Then, before it goes live

```bash
node scripts/verify-video-urls.mjs
```

Run it on a machine that can reach YouTube (this build environment can't). It
confirms every video resolves, prints its **real title and channel** so a
wrong-but-valid ID gets caught, and fails if any reuse is left.
