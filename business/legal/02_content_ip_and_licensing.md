# Legal · Content IP & Licensing (DRAFT)

> **⚠️ Founder draft, not legal advice — review with a NZ IP lawyer.** This covers
> two things: (a) protecting *your* work so it can't be copied, and (b) the
> **serious copyright risk in the other direction** — using material you don't own.

---

## Part A — Protecting your work

### 1. You own your original material
Everything you originally create for Mindcast — the 52-week structure, the
session scripts, the reframes, the workbook, the app, the brand — is **your
copyright** the moment it's written (in NZ, no registration needed for copyright).
Keep records (dated drafts, this repo's git history is excellent evidence of
authorship and dates).

### 2. Members get a limited personal licence — not ownership
Add this to your Terms and the workbook:
> The Mindcast materials, sessions and app are provided to members for their own
> **personal, non-commercial use only**. You may not record, copy, reproduce,
> distribute, resell, publicly perform, or use them to run your own group or
> programme, without Mindcast's written permission. All intellectual property
> remains owned by Mindcast.

### 3. Practical protections
- **No recording** of sessions by attendees (state it; it also protects members'
  privacy).
- **Watermark / footer** the workbook and handouts with a copyright line:
  *"© [Year] Mindcast. For personal use only. Not to be copied or redistributed."*
- **Staff/volunteer IP assignment** — contracts (see [05](../05_hr_and_safeguarding.md))
  must assign any contributions to Mindcast so improvements belong to the business.
- **Trademark** the name/logo with **IPONZ** once you can afford it (~a few
  hundred dollars) — this protects the brand for licensing.

### 4. Register the brand & domain
- Company/trust name, `mindcast.co.nz`, social handles — lock them down.
- Trademark "Mindcast" (word + logo) in the relevant IPONZ classes (education,
  software, community services) before you license to other cities.

## Part B — ⚠️ Copyright risk in the material you *use* (READ THIS)

Your lessons draw on **the Dao / Tao Te Ching** and **A Course in Miracles
(ACIM)**, reframed to remove deity language. Copyright status is very different
for each:

- **Tao Te Ching / classical Daoist texts** — the originals are **public domain**
  (ancient). ✅ You can freely draw on the *ideas* and public-domain translations.
  But **specific modern translations can themselves be copyrighted** — don't copy a
  particular contemporary translation's wording; use public-domain translations or
  your own paraphrase.
- **A Course in Miracles** — **⚠️ this is modern, in-copyright material.** Its text
  is owned/controlled by the **Foundation for Inner Peace** and has a history of
  **aggressive copyright enforcement**. **Do not quote, reproduce, or closely
  paraphrase ACIM text** in your lessons, workbook, or app.

### What you must do
1. **Use ideas, not text.** Copyright protects *expression*, not *ideas*. You can
   be *inspired by* a concept (e.g. "the stories we tell ourselves shape our
   reality") and express it **entirely in your own words** — you cannot lift or
   lightly reword ACIM's actual sentences.
2. **Don't name-drop as a source of authority** in a way that implies licence or
   endorsement. Since you've already removed deity language and reframed
   everything as the person's *own inner wisdom*, lean fully into that: the
   material is **"Mindcast's own synthesis, inspired by many wisdom traditions"** —
   not "the teachings of ACIM."
3. **Audit the 52 weeks** for any phrase that tracks ACIM (or any copyrighted
   book) too closely, and rewrite it in your own voice.
4. **Attribute public-domain and freely-licensed sources** properly; keep a
   private source list showing your material is independently authored.
5. **When in doubt, get an IP lawyer to review** the curriculum before wide
   distribution or licensing — licensing multiplies the exposure.

> Bottom line: your reframing approach (own words, own synthesis, no deity, no
> direct quotes) is exactly the right instinct and your best protection. The risk
> is only if actual copyrighted **wording** survives anywhere. Treat ACIM
> specifically as **"inspiration only, never quoted."**

## Part C — Licensing to other cities (the Year-2 model)
Your long-term plan is to license the lessons + software to facilitators
elsewhere. Set it up so it's clean and protects the mission:

- **Own the IP in a company** (see structure options in [01](../01_vision_and_strategy.md)),
  separate from the local community entity.
- **Licence, don't sell.** A written **Licence Agreement** grants a local operator
  the right to run Mindcast in their town under conditions:
  - Pay a licence fee / revenue share.
  - **Must uphold** the values, safeguarding, vetting, and anti-cult charter
    (this protects the brand from a bad local operator).
  - No sub-licensing; no modifying core content; quality standards; training +
    certification of their facilitators.
  - Territory + term; termination rights if they breach values/safeguarding.
  - Use of brand/trademark under strict guidelines.
- **Certify facilitators** — a paid training + accreditation is itself a revenue
  stream and a quality gate.

## Decisions for your lawyer

  wording **before** launch and **definitely** before licensing.
- Which entity owns the IP (company recommended).
- Trademark timing + classes at IPONZ.
- Licence agreement template for future cities.
