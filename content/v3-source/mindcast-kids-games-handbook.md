# Mindcast — Kids Games Handbook

*52 games, one per week, matched to the child track theme. Ages 3–13, indoor, 600m² hall.*

---

## Before you use any of these

**Nobody is ever out.** Not one of these games eliminates a child. In a programme whose value is *everyone deserves a path to belong*, a game that sends children to the side to watch teaches the opposite of the session. If you adapt a game, keep that rule.

**Two safety-checked adults in the room, always.** Ratio warning applies before the game starts, not after.

**No blindfolds.** Several classic versions of these games use them. Use eyes-closed, or hands-over-eyes, so a child can open their eyes the instant they want to.

**No adult physically posing, lifting or moving a child.** Where a game involves shaping or sculpting, children pose themselves and copy each other.

**Mixed ages: pair, don't split.** A 12-year-old buddying a 4-year-old is doing week 41's work — teaching what you know — a year early. Use it deliberately.

**A child may always sit out and come back.** Watching is a legitimate way to take part. Do not coax, and do not comment on it afterwards.

**Under-5 adaptations are listed for every game.** The rule underneath them all: fewer steps, shorter distance, no elimination, no abstract discussion, and let them do the physical part that the older children only talk about.

---

## Kit list

Everything below is covered by this list. No hoops fixed to walls, no goals, no mats beyond a few soft ones.

**Buy once:** 20 beanbags · 20 cones · 8 flat hoops · 4 soft foam balls · set of skittles · 2 sets building blocks (large, soft) · floor tape (low-tack) · 6 soft mats or towels · parachute (optional, useful) · speaker · hand drum · bell · kitchen timer · shaker · torch · dress-up box (hats, scarves, coats in assorted sizes) · large plastic cups × 40 · jugs · large counters or buttons · air-dry clay or a sand tray · dried beans, cotton wool · rice and sealed bottles · paper plates · sticky labels · markers · pencils · card · a long soft rope.

**Per-session consumables:** paper leaves, station cards, stickers or stamps, checklist cards.

## Movement 1 · See Clearly

### Week 1 — Signal in the Static
*The Signal and the Noise*

**How to play.** Everyone hums, chatters and shuffles — that's the noise. One child hides a beeping timer or shaker somewhere in the hall. The room has to go quiet enough, in stages, to hear it and find it. Repeat with the noise starting louder each time.

**Why it fits.** You cannot hear the quiet thing until you stop making noise.

**Equipment.** Kitchen timer or a shaker; something to hide it under

**Under-5s.** Under-5s are the noise-makers and the finders. Hide it in plain sight and let them 'find' it. Skip the staged quiet — just loud, then whisper, then silent.

---

### Week 2 — Label Tag
*The Stories We Carry*

**How to play.** Each child wears a sticky label on their back with a made-up word (Sparkly, Slow, Loud, Bossy). Others treat them as that label for two minutes. Then everyone guesses their own label, peels it off and says one true thing about themselves instead.

**Why it fits.** Being told what you are, and choosing to take it off.

**Equipment.** Sticky labels, marker

**Under-5s.** Give under-5s only kind or silly labels (Bouncy, Giggly). Keep it to 30 seconds and help them peel it off.

---

### Week 3 — Freeze in the Gap
*The Pattern Interrupt*

**How to play.** Music plays and everyone moves. When it stops, freeze — but before you freeze you must take one big breath. Facilitator calls a feeling ('cross!', 'excited!') and children move like that, then freeze and breathe again.

**Why it fits.** The breath is the gap between the feeling and what you do.

**Equipment.** Speaker

**Under-5s.** Under-5s just do stop-and-go with a big breath. Drop the feelings round or use only two: happy and sleepy.

---

### Week 4 — Body Weather Report
*What Your Body Is Telling You*

**How to play.** Children move around. Facilitator calls a scenario ('you're about to do a speech', 'you're getting a present'). Everyone freezes and puts a hand where they feel it — tummy, chest, head, hands. Then a quick show of hands: who felt it in the tummy?

**Why it fits.** The body answers before the words do.

**Equipment.** None

**Under-5s.** Use only physical scenarios for under-5s — running fast, being tickled, being cold. Ask them to point to where it feels wobbly.

---

### Week 5 — Kind Mirror
*The Mirror — How We See Ourselves*

**How to play.** Pairs face each other. One moves slowly, the other mirrors. Swap. Then each says one true, kind thing they noticed about their partner — how they moved, how carefully they copied.

**Why it fits.** Seeing someone properly, and saying it out loud.

**Equipment.** None

**Under-5s.** Adult partners a under-5 and mirrors them, not the other way round. Big slow movements only.

---

### Week 6 — The Same Cup
*The Comparison Loop*

**How to play.** Each child gets an identical cup with a different amount of water. They compare, complain, negotiate — then discover that pouring into one big jug fills it exactly the same either way.

**Why it fits.** Comparing amounts stops mattering when you see the whole.

**Equipment.** Plastic cups, a large jug, water, towel

**Under-5s.** Under-5s just pour, which they will love. Do it on a mat and expect spills. Skip the discussion.

---

### Week 7 — Boo Ball / Cheer Ball
*The Inner Critic*

**How to play.** Two soft balls. The 'boo ball' is passed while the room says something grumpy and unhelpful ('you'll never do it'). The 'cheer ball' is passed with something honest and kind. Pass them at the same time and notice which one people want.

**Why it fits.** The mean voice and the kind one are both just voices.

**Equipment.** Two soft balls

**Under-5s.** Under-5s hold only the cheer ball and pass it with a cheer. Do not give them the boo ball at all.

---

### Week 8 — Iceberg Hunt
*Beneath the Surface — Emotions as Data*

**How to play.** Big feelings are written on cones at one end (ANGRY, GRUMPY, SHOUTY). Underneath each, hidden in a beanbag pile, are the small feelings (worried, left out, tired, hungry). Teams race to find what's underneath their big feeling.

**Why it fits.** The big feeling is sitting on a smaller one.

**Equipment.** Cones, beanbags, cards

**Under-5s.** Under-5s sort two piles: big feeling face, little feeling face. Use printed faces, not words.

---

### Week 9 — Safe Hands Relay
*The Wounds We Carry*

**How to play.** Children carry something fragile — an egg, a full cup, a tower of blocks — across the hall and hand it to a named safe grown-up at the other end. The point of the game is the handover, not the speed.

**Why it fits.** When something is hard to carry, you hand it to a safe grown-up.

**Equipment.** Plastic eggs or blocks, cups

**Under-5s.** Under-5s carry a soft toy with two hands and hand it over. Shorten the distance to five metres. Always end with the handover.

---

### Week 10 — Mask Off
*The Masks We Wear*

**How to play.** Each child holds a paper plate with a big smile drawn on it in front of their face and answers 'how are you?' from behind it. Then they lower it and answer again. No one has to say anything different — but they can.

**Why it fits.** Sometimes the face and the feeling are not the same.

**Equipment.** Paper plates, markers, sticks

**Under-5s.** Under-5s just play peekaboo with the plate. Ask 'happy face or sad face?' and let them show you.

---

### Week 11 — The Backpack Game
*Seeing Others Clearly*

**How to play.** Volunteers wear a backpack loaded with soft objects, each one labelled with something invisible ('didn't sleep', 'lost my lunch', 'moved house'). They try to do a simple task — hop, catch, skip. The room guesses what might be in the bag.

**Why it fits.** Everyone is carrying something you cannot see.

**Equipment.** Backpack, soft objects, labels

**Under-5s.** Under-5s wear a nearly empty backpack and try to skip. Keep the load light and the task short.

---

### Week 12 — Loop Track
*The Habit Loop — How We Got Here*

**How to play.** Chalk or tape a loop on the floor with three stations: TRIGGER, WHAT I DO, WHAT I GET. Children walk their own loop for a habit (bored → tablet → fun) and then try walking a different second station.

**Why it fits.** A habit is a track you walk without choosing.

**Equipment.** Floor tape or chalk, station signs

**Under-5s.** Under-5s just walk the loop holding an adult's hand and stamp at each station. Name the stations aloud for them.

---

### Week 13 — Treasure Map Review
*Integration — What We Now See*

**How to play.** Thirteen stations around the hall, one per week, each with a picture from that session. Children move around collecting a stamp and saying one thing they remember. Ends with the whole map on the wall.

**Why it fits.** Looking back at everything we noticed.

**Equipment.** Station cards, stamps or stickers, wall map

**Under-5s.** Under-5s collect stickers with a buddy. Six stations, not thirteen.

---

## Movement 2 · Unlearn

### Week 14 — Red Light, Own Light
*The Permission You're Still Waiting For*

**How to play.** Standard red light / green light, but after three rounds the facilitator stops calling. Children have to decide themselves when to move. Some freeze waiting. Talk about waiting for permission that isn't coming.

**Why it fits.** Nobody is coming to say go.

**Equipment.** None

**Under-5s.** Under-5s play normal red light green light. Do the no-caller round for ten seconds only, with an adult modelling moving.

---

### Week 15 — Costume Swap
*Letting Go of Who You Were Supposed to Be*

**How to play.** A pile of dress-ups. Children are told to dress as what other people expect them to be, walk the hall, then swap into what they'd actually pick. Two laps, two feelings.

**Why it fits.** Wearing what's expected, then choosing.

**Equipment.** Dress-up box, hats, scarves

**Under-5s.** Under-5s just do the second lap — dress as whatever they like and parade. Skip the first lap entirely.

---

### Week 16 — Heavy Beanbags
*The Forgiveness Loop*

**How to play.** Each grudge is a beanbag. Children name something they're cross about (or make one up) and add a beanbag to their arms. Then they try to catch, hug or high-five while holding them all. They can put one down whenever they like.

**Why it fits.** Staying cross is heavy, and putting it down is your choice.

**Equipment.** Beanbags

**Under-5s.** Under-5s hold two soft toys and try to clap. Let them drop them and cheer. No naming required.

---

### Week 17 — Do-Over Station
*Forgiving Yourself*

**How to play.** Skittles or a bean-bag toss. Everyone misses on purpose first. Then everyone gets a do-over and a friendly line to say to themselves: 'that's okay, go again.' Nobody keeps score.

**Why it fits.** Getting it wrong, and being kind about it.

**Equipment.** Skittles or cones, beanbags

**Under-5s.** Under-5s get unlimited do-overs and a big cheer for every throw. Move the line closer.

---

### Week 18 — Coach in Your Ear
*Rewiring the Critic — Building the Inner Coach*

**How to play.** One child does a simple challenge (balance on a line, bounce a ball ten times). One partner plays the mean voice quietly, one plays the honest kind coach. Swap. Which one helped?

**Why it fits.** You can choose which voice coaches you.

**Equipment.** Balls, floor tape

**Under-5s.** For under-5s, only the coach role exists. An adult cheers them along a balance line. Never let a small child be the mean voice.

---

### Week 19 — Yes/No Islands
*Breaking the People-Pleasing Pattern*

**How to play.** Two hoops: YES and NO. Facilitator calls out things ('want to be first?', 'want a hug from someone you don't know?', 'want to share your snack?'). Children jump to their honest answer. No one is allowed to comment on anyone else's island.

**Why it fits.** Your answer is allowed to be no.

**Equipment.** Two hoops or floor tape

**Under-5s.** Under-5s answer with thumbs up or down while sitting. Use only easy, safe questions.

---

### Week 20 — The Never-Ending Cheer
*Unlearning Scarcity — There Is Enough*

**How to play.** One child is cheered by the room. Then two. Then everyone at once. The room notices that the cheering did not run out — unlike a bag of sweets, which is passed around alongside it and does.

**Why it fits.** Some things run out. Kindness doesn't.

**Equipment.** A small bag of something shareable

**Under-5s.** Under-5s get cheered first and loudest. Keep it to one round each.

---

### Week 21 — Follow the Leader Chain
*The Relationships That Shaped You*

**How to play.** A leader starts a movement, the line copies. Every 30 seconds the leader goes to the back and a new one starts. At the end: which move did you keep doing even after that leader was gone?

**Why it fits.** We keep doing what the people ahead of us did.

**Equipment.** Speaker

**Under-5s.** Under-5s go near the front where the movement is clearest. Give them a turn as leader and copy them enthusiastically.

---

### Week 22 — Fortress
*Setting Down the Armour*

**How to play.** Children build a wall around themselves from soft blocks or cushions, then try to pass a ball to a friend through it. Then they take one block out. Then another. How much wall do you actually need?

**Why it fits.** Walls keep things out, including the good things.

**Equipment.** Soft blocks, cushions, soft ball

**Under-5s.** Under-5s build a small wall and knock it down, which is the whole game for them. Cheer the knocking down.

---

### Week 23 — Autumn Leaves
*Grief and Growth — What Endings Make Possible*

**How to play.** Everyone holds a paper leaf with something that ended written or drawn on it. On a signal they let them fall. Then the leaves are swept into a pile and seeds or paper flowers are planted in it.

**Why it fits.** When something ends it makes space.

**Equipment.** Paper leaves, markers, paper flowers

**Under-5s.** Under-5s draw on their leaf and drop it. Let them do the sweeping — they will want to.

---

### Week 24 — Burnt Toast Alarm
*Rewiring Fear — From Threat to Signal*

**How to play.** A loud alarm goes off at random. Children freeze, then check: is it a fire, or is it burnt toast? A card is revealed each time — most say burnt toast. They practise the check before reacting.

**Why it fits.** Not every alarm is an emergency.

**Equipment.** Bell or buzzer, picture cards

**Under-5s.** Under-5s just freeze and do a big breath when the bell rings. Skip the cards; use a gentle bell, not a buzzer.

---

### Week 25 — Old Coat
*The Stories That No Longer Serve*

**How to play.** Children put on a coat several sizes too small (or a tiny hat) and try to move. It fitted once. Then they swap for one that fits now.

**Why it fits.** Some stories about you don't fit any more.

**Equipment.** Assorted dress-up coats and hats, all sizes

**Under-5s.** Under-5s find the hat that fits from a pile. Turn it into a hat parade.

---

### Week 26 — Backpack Empty-Out
*Integration — What We Have Released*

**How to play.** Children carry a heavy-ish backpack around a course. Then they name one thing to take out, remove a weight, and do the course again. And again. Notice the difference each time.

**Why it fits.** We have put things down this term.

**Equipment.** Backpacks, soft weights or cushions

**Under-5s.** Under-5s carry one item, take it out, and run the short course. Two laps maximum.

---

## Movement 3 · Rebuild

### Week 27 — Build-a-Me Statue
*Who Are You Now? — Rebuilding Identity*

**How to play.** In pairs, one child sculpts the other into a statue of 'who I want to be' — brave, calm, kind. Photograph nothing; just walk the gallery and guess. Then everyone strikes their own chosen pose at once.

**Why it fits.** You get to choose the shape you take.

**Equipment.** None

**Under-5s.** Sculpting means posing themselves, not being moved by someone else. Under-5s copy an adult's pose. No hands-on posing of any child, by anyone.

---

### Week 28 — Rescue the Treasures
*What Do You Actually Value?*

**How to play.** Ten objects scattered in the hall, a rope 'river' across the middle. Teams may only carry three across. They must decide together which three matter most, and say why.

**Why it fits.** When you can't have everything, what do you choose?

**Equipment.** Assorted objects, rope

**Under-5s.** Under-5s rescue one object each and say why they picked it. Skip the group decision.

---

### Week 29 — One Brick at a Time
*The Habits That Build You*

**How to play.** Every child adds one block to a shared tower each time they complete a small action — ten star jumps, one kind word, tying a lace. Fast building is not allowed. One brick, one action.

**Why it fits.** Small things done again and again build something.

**Equipment.** Building blocks or cups

**Under-5s.** Under-5s add a block for every star jump. Big blocks, low tower, lots of cheering.

---

### Week 30 — Foundation Wobble
*Your Body as Foundation*

**How to play.** Teams build the tallest tower they can on a wobbly base (a cushion). Then rebuild on a firm one. Same blocks, different base.

**Why it fits.** What you build sits on how you sleep, eat and move.

**Equipment.** Blocks, cushions, firm boards

**Under-5s.** Under-5s build on the cushion and knock it over. Then build on the floor. Compare heights with them.

---

### Week 31 — Garden Circle
*The People You Choose*

**How to play.** Children plant themselves as 'seeds' around the hall. Some spots are near the music and cheering, some near a grumpy corner. Everyone tries to grow (stretch up) in their spot. Then they choose a new one.

**Why it fits.** You grow differently depending on where you plant yourself.

**Equipment.** Speaker, cones

**Under-5s.** Under-5s go straight to the cheering corner and grow. Skip the grumpy corner entirely for them.

---

### Week 32 — Echo Hall
*How You Speak to Yourself*

**How to play.** One child says something to the far wall; the room echoes it back. Say something harsh — the room echoes it harshly. Say something kind — same. The room only gives back what was sent.

**Why it fits.** What you say to yourself comes back.

**Equipment.** None

**Under-5s.** Under-5s shout a happy word and hear it echoed. Only kind words for this age group.

---

### Week 33 — Rhythm Course
*Creating Structure That Serves You*

**How to play.** An obstacle course is run in a random order first — chaotic, tiring. Then it's run in a set rhythm with a drumbeat. Same course, different feeling.

**Why it fits.** A rhythm makes the same work easier.

**Equipment.** Cones, hoops, drum or speaker

**Under-5s.** Under-5s only do the rhythm version, holding a partner's hand. Four obstacles maximum.

---

### Week 34 — The Rest Station
*Rest as a Practice, Not a Reward*

**How to play.** A relay where every third lap you must lie down on a mat for 30 seconds. Teams that skip the rest get slower. Teams that rest properly get faster.

**Why it fits.** Rest is part of the game, not a reward at the end.

**Equipment.** Mats or towels, cones

**Under-5s.** Under-5s do the resting station only — lying down, being counted down from ten. They will treat it as the best part.

---

### Week 35 — Junk Orchestra
*Creativity and Expression*

**How to play.** A pile of random objects. In small groups, children have five minutes to make an instrument and a sound. Then a two-minute performance. Nobody is judged and there is no winner.

**Why it fits.** Making something because you want to.

**Equipment.** Containers, rice, elastic bands, boxes, spoons

**Under-5s.** Under-5s shake a filled bottle. Make the bottles in advance and seal them properly.

---

### Week 36 — The Missing Piece
*Purpose — What You're Here For*

**How to play.** Each child is given one puzzle piece or one job (holder, counter, cheerer, fetcher). The task can only be completed if everyone does their bit. Then one job is removed and they try again.

**Why it fits.** What you're good at is needed by someone.

**Equipment.** Large floor puzzle or a group task

**Under-5s.** Under-5s get the cheering job, which is real and visible. Make sure someone notices them doing it.

---

### Week 37 — Ten Buttons
*Money, Abundance and Your Relationship With Resources*

**How to play.** Every child gets ten buttons. Around the hall are stations: things you need (water, food), things you want (toy, treat), and a giving jar. They spend their ten and then talk about it.

**Why it fits.** Money is choices, and some of them are for other people.

**Equipment.** Buttons or counters, station cards, a jar

**Under-5s.** Under-5s get three buttons and two stations: something for me, something for someone else. Watch for choking hazards — use large counters.

---

### Week 38 — The Hive
*The Community You Build*

**How to play.** The room has to move a pile of beanbags from one end to the other, but each child may only use one specific action (roll, pass, carry, throw, catch). Nobody can do the whole job alone.

**Why it fits.** We each bring one thing, and together it works.

**Equipment.** Beanbags, cones

**Under-5s.** Under-5s get the carrying job with a partner. Shorten the distance.

---

### Week 39 — Then and Now
*Integration — Who You Are Becoming*

**How to play.** Children re-run one game from earlier in the year — week 3's freeze game works well — and notice what's different about how they play it now.

**Why it fits.** You are not the same as you were.

**Equipment.** Whatever the chosen game needs

**Under-5s.** Under-5s repeat week 3's stop-and-go. Ask them if it feels easier.

---

## Movement 4 · Live It

### Week 40 — Helping Hands Relay
*From Self-Development to Service*

**How to play.** A course that cannot be completed alone — one child is 'stuck' at each station and must be helped past by the next runner. The runner who helps most is the point, not the fastest.

**Why it fits.** What you've learned is for other people too.

**Equipment.** Cones, hoops

**Under-5s.** Under-5s are the ones helped, paired with an older buddy. Then swap so they get to help too.

---

### Week 41 — Teach Me That
*Teaching What You've Learned*

**How to play.** Every child teaches one thing they can do — a clap pattern, a stretch, a knot, a joke — to two other people. Then those two teach it on. Count how far one skill travels around the hall.

**Why it fits.** You know it better once you've taught it.

**Equipment.** None

**Under-5s.** Under-5s teach a clap or a silly face. Adults learn it from them with obvious enthusiasm.

---

### Week 42 — Spotlight, One Step
*The Courage to Be Seen*

**How to play.** A torch spotlight on the floor. Children step into it for as long as they want — one second is fine — and the room gives a warm cheer. Nobody has to speak. Nobody may be pushed in.

**Why it fits.** Being seen is nicer than it is scary.

**Equipment.** Torch or lamp

**Under-5s.** Under-5s go in pairs holding hands. Cheer even harder. Never coax a child who says no.

---

### Week 43 — The Hard Right
*Living Your Values Under Pressure*

**How to play.** A simple relay with a shortcut clearly available. Nobody is watching that lane. Afterwards, hands up honestly — who took it? No consequences, just the conversation.

**Why it fits.** Doing the right thing when nobody would know.

**Equipment.** Cones, floor tape

**Under-5s.** Under-5s run the course with an adult. Skip the shortcut and the discussion; just run and cheer.

---

### Week 44 — Knot Untie
*Conflict as a Growth Practice*

**How to play.** A group holds hands in a tangle and has to untangle without letting go. Requires talking, patience and not walking off. Facilitator names the moment someone nearly gives up.

**Why it fits.** Staying in the tangle is how it gets sorted.

**Equipment.** None

**Under-5s.** Under-5s do a three-person tangle with an adult. Fifteen seconds, then untangle and cheer. Hand-holding only with peers or a rostered adult.

---

### Week 45 — Give-Away Relay
*Generosity as a Way of Being*

**How to play.** Each child starts with five beanbags and must finish with none — but they can only give them away, never drop them. Some children end up with piles. Then everyone gives again.

**Why it fits.** Giving is more fun than holding.

**Equipment.** Beanbags

**Under-5s.** Under-5s give one beanbag to someone and get a thank you. That's the whole game.

---

### Week 46 — Full Cup
*Enough — Living From Sufficiency*

**How to play.** Each child fills a cup with water to where 'enough' feels right. Then they try to carry it across the hall. The over-fillers spill. Then they try again with less.

**Why it fits.** Enough is easier to carry than more.

**Equipment.** Cups, water, towels, mats

**Under-5s.** Under-5s use rice or beads instead of water. Same lesson, no mopping.

---

### Week 47 — Sprouting Seeds
*The Long Game — Patience and Persistence*

**How to play.** Beans planted in cotton wool at week 47, checked every week to week 52. The game is a relay to plant and water them. Nothing visible happens for days.

**Why it fits.** Roots come before the shoot.

**Equipment.** Beans, cotton wool, cups, water

**Under-5s.** Under-5s do the planting and the watering, which is the good part. Give them their own labelled cup.

---

### Week 48 — Ripple Tag
*Leaving People Better Than You Found Them*

**How to play.** A calm tag where being tagged means receiving a compliment, not being out. The tagged child then goes and compliments two others. Watch how fast the whole hall is tagged.

**Why it fits.** Small good moments spread further than you think.

**Equipment.** None

**Under-5s.** Under-5s are tagged first so they get to do the spreading. Keep the compliments simple: 'you're fun'.

---

### Week 49 — Sand Handprints
*Your Legacy — What You're Building Beyond Yourself*

**How to play.** Each child presses a handprint into a shared tray of sand or dough, then adds one word for what they want to leave behind. The tray goes on the wall.

**Why it fits.** You leave a mark on people.

**Equipment.** Air-dry clay or a sand tray, board

**Under-5s.** Under-5s do the handprint and say their word out loud for an adult to write. Supervise dough closely.

---

### Week 50 — Ten Good Things Scavenger Hunt
*Gratitude as a Practice, Not a Feeling*

**How to play.** Teams find ten specific good things in the hall or in each other — someone's laugh, a good pass, a kind word, a warm coat. Not objects. Noticings.

**Why it fits.** The more you look, the more you find.

**Equipment.** Checklist cards, pencils

**Under-5s.** Under-5s find three things with a buddy, using pictures rather than words on the card.

---

### Week 51 — The Never-Finished Tower
*The Ongoing Practice — There Is No Arrival*

**How to play.** Build a tower. When it gets tall, add one more block. Then one more. It falls, and you start again. The game has no end and no winner. Play until time runs out.

**Why it fits.** There's no finish line. That's the good bit.

**Equipment.** Blocks

**Under-5s.** Under-5s knock it down and rebuild endlessly, which is exactly the point. Do not stop them.

---

### Week 52 — The Whole Year Trail
*Integration — A Year of Becoming*

**How to play.** Fifty-two stations is too many — pick thirteen, one per month-ish, each with an object from that session. Children walk the trail and say one thing at any station they choose. Ends with the whole room in a circle.

**Why it fits.** Looking back at all of it.

**Equipment.** Objects from the year, station cards

**Under-5s.** Under-5s walk the trail with an adult and pick up the objects. Six stations. Let them carry something home.

---
