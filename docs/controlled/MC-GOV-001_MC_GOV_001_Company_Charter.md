# Company Charter
**Mindcast Limited · Taupō, Aotearoa New Zealand**
MC-GOV-001 · Version 1.2 · August 2026 · Governance
Why Mindcast exists, what it promises, and the boundaries it will not cross to grow. Every other policy in the controlled document set is downstream of this document.

**Applies to:** Whole organisation

## 1 · Who we are
Mindcast Limited is a New Zealand company incorporated on 26 March 2026, based in Taupō. It operates Mindcast — a weekly, in-person, facilitated gathering for personal development, running a 52-week curriculum across three parallel tracks: adults, teens and children.
We are a membership community, not a course business. Membership revenue covers the room, the people, the tools and the operating margin required to keep the business sustainable. The live Sunday gathering may be attended free; paid membership unlocks the interactive app experience and the continuing digital journey. Revenue exists to sustain and improve the gathering, while Mindcast Limited remains a transparent for-profit business.
---
## 2 · Purpose
**To help people move from unconscious patterns, to conscious awareness, to meaningful change — and to create communities where that change is practised together rather than consumed alone.**
**You are unaware, and you don't change.**
**You are aware, and you still don't change.**
**You are aware — and you do.**
**Mindcast is the room built for that third one.**
**UNCONSCIOUS → CONSCIOUS → CHANGED**
The problem we exist to solve is not a lack of information. It is the implementation gap between becoming aware of something and actually changing what you do. People can understand the pattern, finish the book, listen to the podcast and still live the same way on Friday. Content is abundant and follow-through is scarce. Mindcast supplies the missing half — structure, rhythm, action, witness, community, and a return date.
---
## 3 · Mission
**Mindcast turns awareness into action and insight into lived change. We curate the best of what has already been said, turn it into questions that make it personal, and give people a safe, structured weekly practice to understand themselves, take meaningful action and grow alongside a real community.**
Every week, the method is simple: **Notice it. Name it. Do it.**
- **Notice it** — see the pattern, reaction or behaviour that may have been operating unconsciously.
- **Name it** — put clear language around what is happening, what it is costing, and what needs to change.
- **Do it** — choose one meaningful action and practise something different in real life.
Awareness is necessary, but it is not the finish line. The purpose of the weekly practice is movement from **unconscious → conscious → changed**.
We deliver this through:
- **A weekly live gathering** — one theme, one facilitator, one screen, one hour.
- **A private reflection** — journalling that belongs to the member and no one else.
- **A midweek Life Group** — a small group that notices when you do not turn up.
- **A 52-week curriculum** — sequenced across four phases, running in parallel for adults, teens and children so whole families grow side by side.
- **A commitment loop** — set an intention, come back in seven days, and be gently held to it.

## 4 · Vision
**A world where every neighbourhood has a room you can walk into and become more yourself — together, and where the next generation grows up already holding the tools their parents had to go looking for.**
Taupō is the prototype. Once one full 52-week cycle is proven, documented and safe, the model may be licensed to facilitators in other towns, with curriculum, software, brand and safeguarding standards licensed from the centre.

## 5 · Brand promise and behaviour framework
**Brand promise:** Tune into yourself.
**Transformation:** Unconscious → Conscious → Changed.
**Behaviour framework:** Notice it. Name it. Do it.
The transformation describes the journey Mindcast exists to support. The behaviour framework is the weekly method that helps make that journey practical: notice what is happening, name it clearly, then do something different. The brand promise carries the feeling; the behaviour framework carries the method. Application rules are set out in the Brand Guidelines (MC-BRD-001).

## 6 · Values
**Community over content.** The room, relationships and repeated practice matter more than producing more information for people to consume.
**Autonomy over dependence.** A successful participant becomes more capable of noticing and choosing for themselves, not more dependent on Mindcast, its founder or facilitators.
**Draw out, do not preach.** Facilitators help people explore their own experience. Mindcast does not impose a belief system, deity, doctrine or dogma, and does not present any leader as the source of truth.
**Safe by design.** Safeguarding, privacy, professional boundaries, moderation and health and safety are part of the product — not administrative extras.
**Action over passive consumption.** Each week should help participants move from awareness toward one realistic action or intention they can practise.
**Everyone deserves a path to belong.** Free attendance and accessible bridge products sit alongside paid membership. Pricing and programme design aim to reduce unnecessary barriers to participation while keeping the organisation sustainable. Concessions may be offered permanently, never as pressure-based promotions.
**Honest about what we are.** Mindcast is not therapy, medicine, a church or a cure. We are explicit about our scope and refer people to qualified professional help when a need sits outside it. Not every week will land for every person, and we say so.
**No pressure, ever.** We do not use urgency, scarcity, guilt or manipulation to recruit or retain anybody.

## 7 · What we will not do
These are hard limits. They bind the founder, the board, every employee, contractor, volunteer and future licensee.
- We will not position any individual — including the founder — as a guru, healer, teacher or spiritual authority.
- We will not provide therapy, counselling, diagnosis, medical advice or crisis intervention. We refer.
- We will not discourage a member from leaving, use retention pressure after cancellation, or make departure socially costly.
- We will not ask members for donations beyond clearly published commercial fees, or use escalating payment tiers designed to exploit belonging.
- We will not ask anyone to reduce contact with family, friends, work or other communities.
- We will not display, publish or repurpose anything a member writes without explicit, specific, revocable consent.
- We will not sell, rent or trade member data, or use member reflections to train third-party AI models.
- We will not accept sponsorship or partnership that requires promoting a product to members.

## 8 · Business model
**Core engine — paid memberships.** Adult, teen and family memberships pay for interactive app participation, saved reflections, digital workbook and history, member tools, and the operational system surrounding the weekly gathering.
**Free community access.** The live Sunday session is open to attend free, subject to venue capacity and registration. A non-member may listen and take part in the room but does not receive ongoing app access or saved interactive participation.
**Bridge product.** A non-member may buy the day's physical worksheet for **$5 NZD**. This is a low-friction bridge, not the primary revenue engine.
**Founding offer.** Working commercial assumption: first 100 adult memberships at **$29 NZD/week**, price locked for 12 months; standard adult membership **$35 NZD/week** thereafter. Youth and family pricing is approved separately before launch.
**Products.** Printed workbooks, session handouts, and replacement or additional NFC bracelets.
**Licensing — after proof.** Curriculum, software and brand standards may be licensed to facilitators in other towns only after Taupō has completed a safe and documented full cycle and governance approves the model.
**Cost drivers.** Venue, paid facilitators and session staff, insurance, software and infrastructure, printing, vetting, training, professional review, and administration.
**Financial principle.** Core operational roles should become paid as early as practicable. Genuine volunteers may support peripheral community activity, but unpaid labour must not become the economic foundation of the for-profit operating model.

## 9 · Legal and structural position
Mindcast Limited holds the intellectual property, software, contracts and licensing rights. The founder is the sole director at incorporation.
An open structural decision is whether a local community arm should ever sit inside a separate charitable trust or incorporated society while Mindcast Limited licenses curriculum and software to it. This is **not** the current operating model and must not blur the fact that Mindcast Limited is a transparent for-profit business.
Any future community-arm structure must be decided with a New Zealand lawyer and accountant before grant applications or a first licensee. Until then Mindcast operates wholly as a limited company.

## 10 · Governance commitments
- Independent Advisory Group of at least three people, none employees or family of the founder, meeting at least twice a year.
- Named Safeguarding Lead who is not the founder once paid facilitators are engaged, plus a Deputy.
- Annual member-facing summary of where membership income went, complaints and incidents at aggregate level, and changes made as a result.
- Open complaints route that does not run through the person complained about.
- Guests, family, advisors and media may observe sessions on request where privacy and safeguarding allow.
---
## 11 · Strategic test for decisions
When making a product, staffing, marketing or commercial decision, ask:
1. Does this strengthen the weekly practice and real community?
1. Does it protect participant autonomy and safety?
1. Does it make follow-through more likely rather than simply adding more content?
1. Can we explain it transparently to members, parents, staff and partners?
1. Does it fit the actual scope of Mindcast?
1. Would we be comfortable if a member read the internal reasoning behind this decision?
---
## 12 · Founder notes
**What should never change as Mindcast scales?**
Participant autonomy, the anti-guru structure, and safeguarding. The curriculum, the format and the technology are all replaceable. These three are not.
**What may change as technology and delivery evolve?**
Delivery format, curation sources, pricing model, the software, and whether Mindcast operates in one town or twenty.
**Which value wins when commercial growth conflicts with participant safety or trust?**
Safety and trust, without exception. A single safeguarding or trust failure ends the organisation. Slower growth only delays it.
---
## 13 · How this charter is used
Every policy, contract, product decision and piece of marketing is tested against this document. If a proposal conflicts with Section 7, the proposal is dropped regardless of the revenue attached to it.
This charter is reviewed annually. Any amendment to Sections 6 or 7 is recorded in writing with the reason and notified to members.
> **Professional review.** The structure, employment, participant, privacy and safeguarding documents must receive appropriate New Zealand professional review before they are relied on in production.
