## Safeguarding & Child Protection Policy
How Mindcast keeps children, young people and vulnerable adults safe. This is the policy that everything else depends on. Read it before your first session, not after.
**Document owner:** Safeguarding Lead  
**Applies to:** Everyone — staff, contractors, volunteers, board, members  
**Next review:** February 2027  
**Status:** Live — annual review mandatory
> **Immediate danger: call 111.**  
Child-safety concern: Oranga Tamariki **0508 326 459**.  
Person in distress: **1737** — call or text.  
You do not need permission from anyone at Mindcast to make an emergency or statutory-safety call.
## 1 · Scope and principles
This policy applies across every Mindcast session, Life Group, event and online interaction.
1. **A child's safety comes before everything** — reputation, relationships and organisational discomfort.
1. **Workers report concerns; they do not decide whether they are serious enough.**
1. **We report what we see or hear, not what we can prove.** Investigation belongs to statutory authorities.
1. Children and young people are told who they can talk to, that concerns will be taken seriously, and that raising one will not get them in trouble.
1. **Everyone is checked** where the role requires it — including friends, family and the founder.
1. Concerns about a worker or the founder go through an independent route and never through the person complained about.
The legal framework to be monitored includes the Children's Act 2014, Oranga Tamariki Act 1989, Crimes Act 1961, Privacy Act 2020, Health and Safety at Work Act 2015 and applicable safety-check regulations. The Professional & Legal Review Register is the source of truth for legal updates.
## 2 · Who is responsible
**Safeguarding Lead** — receives concerns, manages reporting, holds the restricted vetting register, oversees training and records. Once paid facilitators are engaged, this role must not be the founder.
**Deputy Safeguarding Lead** — acts when the Lead is unavailable or is the subject of the concern.
**Founder / Director** — resources the system and steps back entirely from any concern involving them.
**Independent Advisory Group** — at least three independent people; receives concerns about the founder or Safeguarding Lead and provides an external governance route.
**Every worker and volunteer** — reports every concern the same day. No exceptions.
Names/direct contacts are published on the website, session noticeboard and member/guardian induction material once appointments are complete.
## 3 · Safety checking
No one works or volunteers with children, young people or vulnerable adults until the required safety check is **complete and cleared**.
For a children's-worker role, complete the legally required components including identity, Police vetting where applicable, work history, relevant registration information, referee interviews/checks, interview and written risk assessment. Re-check at least every three years where required, or earlier under Mindcast policy/risk assessment.
A Police vet is an input, not the complete suitability decision. A person with a disqualifying/specified conviction must not be placed in a restricted role unless the applicable lawful exemption/process is satisfied.
Everyone must promptly disclose a charge, conviction, investigation or professional finding relevant to working with children or vulnerable people.
## 4 · Behaviour rules
### Never
- Be alone and out of sight with a child or young person who is not your own. If unavoidable for immediate safety, remain visible and report it afterwards.
- Privately message, text, call, video-call, email, follow or add a young member on personal social media.
- Transport a young person alone outside an approved arrangement.
- Give a young member money, gifts, food or a lift outside an agreed supervised arrangement.
- Photograph/video a child without the required written guardian consent.
- Use physical contact beyond what is appropriate, welcome and proportionate.
- Discuss your own romantic/sexual life with a young person.
- Promise secrecy to a child.
### Always
- Use **two safety-checked adults** for kids/teen sessions and any other role where the approved risk assessment requires it.
- Use shared/logged channels for young-member communications, with guardian visibility where required.
- Report any necessary boundary exception to the Safeguarding Lead the same day.
- Model the boundary consistently, including outside the room.
If a young person offers personal contact details, sends a social request or asks to talk privately, decline warmly, explain the boundary and notify the Safeguarding Lead where appropriate.
## 5 · Recognising concern
Workers are not diagnosing; they are noticing. Concerns may be physical, emotional/behavioural, neglect-related, direct/partial disclosures, or concerning adult behaviour such as secrecy, favouritism, gifts, attempts to isolate or repeated pressure for one-to-one contact.
**Grooming can look like kindness.** That is why the behaviour rules apply even to people we trust and like.
## 6 · If a child discloses to you
### Do
- Stay calm and listen at the child's pace.
- Believe and reassure them that telling was the right thing to do.
- Be honest that you need to tell the person responsible for keeping children safe.
- Use only open prompts such as “Can you tell me more about that?” where necessary.
- Record the child's own words as soon as possible, including date, time, place and who was present.
### Do not
- Promise secrecy.
- Ask leading questions or repeatedly question the child.
- Investigate or try to decide whether the allegation is true.
- show shock/anger in a way that burdens the child.
- Contact the alleged abuser or family before following the safeguarding escalation path where doing so could increase risk or compromise a response.
- Discuss the disclosure beyond the authorised safeguarding route.
## 7 · Escalation path
- **Immediate danger:** call 111, then notify the Safeguarding Lead.
- **Concern about a child's safety:** tell the Safeguarding Lead the same day and create the approved factual record within 24 hours.
- **Concern about a worker/volunteer/founder:** tell the Safeguarding Lead; if the concern is about the Lead, use the Deputy/Independent Advisory Group directly.
- **Mental distress/self-harm risk:** follow MC-SAF-002 Scope of Practice & Distress Escalation. Facilitators refer; they do not treat.
- A person may contact Police/Oranga Tamariki directly whenever they believe that is required; internal permission is not a prerequisite.
## 8 · Session and communication controls
- Two checked adults present for youth sessions.
- Young-member communications use approved logged channels.
- Guardian consent/permissions are recorded through the approved flow.
- Attendance/check-in does not expose a young person's private reflections.
- Kiosk/welcome-wall modes show only the minimum information required.
- Media use requires the specific consent appropriate to the person/guardian.
## 9 · Allegations involving staff
Protect the child/person first. Preserve evidence and records. Do not run an informal internal investigation that compromises a statutory investigation. Employment/process decisions are handled separately under MC-HR-008 with professional advice as needed.
No retaliation is permitted against a person who raises a safeguarding concern in good faith.
## 10 · Records and privacy
Safeguarding records contain the person's own words where relevant and clearly separate observed facts from opinion. Record what action was taken and who was notified.
Safeguarding records are stored **separately from ordinary member/personnel records**, encrypted and restricted to the Safeguarding Lead, Deputy and appropriately authorised governance/response people.
Working retention rule: **minimum seven years**, or longer where an authority/professional adviser directs. Privacy must never be used as a reason not to make a necessary child-safety report.
## 11 · Training
- **Everyone:** this policy, Code of Conduct and disclosure handling — before first session, then annually.
- **Kids/teen track workers:** full safeguarding module, scenarios and grooming recognition — before first session, then annually.
- **Facilitators:** above plus scope-of-practice/distress escalation; role-appropriate external first-aid/mental-health training where approved.
- **Safeguarding Lead:** external safeguarding training before/around appointment and refreshed on the approved cycle.
Training completion is recorded per person in MC-TRN-001 / the Staff Training portal and audited regularly. **Anyone overdue on a member-contact gate stands down from member-facing duties until it is current.**
## 12 · What members and whānau are told
At induction, on the website and at the venue, members/guardians are told:
- who the Safeguarding Lead/Deputy are and how to contact them;
- what safety-checking and adult-behaviour rules apply;
- how to raise a concern, including one about the founder;
- that raising a concern will never affect membership or participation.
## 13 · Review
Review annually and immediately after a serious safeguarding incident or material legal/operational change. The Safeguarding Lead signs off the review and the Independent Advisory Group notes it.
> **External review required:** MC-SAF-001 and MC-SAF-002 should be reviewed by an external safeguarding adviser before the first session with children/young people present. Any legal requirements should also be checked against current New Zealand guidance before launch.
