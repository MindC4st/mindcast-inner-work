## Module 1 · What Mindcast is
**Outcomes:** explain Mindcast to a stranger in 30 seconds · name the values · state the things we will never do.
### The problem
People consume enormous amounts of self-development and act on almost none of it. The gap is implementation. Mindcast supplies structure, rhythm, witness and a return date.
**Purpose:** Mindcast exists to close the gap between knowing and doing.  
**Mission:** To give every town a room where ordinary people become more themselves — together, every week, for as long as they want to keep coming.  
**Vision:** A world where every neighbourhood has a room you can walk into and become more yourself — together.
### How it works
A 52-week curriculum across adults, teens and children. Each week: live facilitated gathering, private reflection and a midweek Life Group. Members set an intention, return in seven days and are gently held to it.
### Values and hard limits
Community over content · draw out, do not preach · everyone can afford to belong · safe by design · honest about what we are · no pressure, ever.
We never position a guru; provide therapy/clinical care; make leaving costly; pressure attendance/payment; repurpose member words without specific consent; sell member data/use reflections to train third-party AI; or isolate people from family/friends/other communities.
### Checkpoints
- **1.1 · Free text:** In your own words, how would you explain Mindcast to someone who asked about it in a café?
- **1.2 · Multi-select:** Which of these is Mindcast? Therapy · a facilitated personal development community · a church · a weekly in-person gathering · a coaching business · a 52-week programme for adults, teens and kids.
- **1.3 · Free text:** A member says “you've changed my life, you're basically my therapist.” What is wrong with accepting that, and what would you say?
- **1.4 · Acknowledgement:** I have read MC-GOV-001 Company Charter and understand its hard limits.
## Module 2 · The room and the weekly ritual
**Outcomes:** run the session flow · know the role/handover points · set the room up safely.
### Ritual
Arrive → NFC check-in → welcome wall → facilitated session → private reflection → Life Group invitation.
**The ritual is the product.** Members are not coming for novelty; they are coming for the reliability of the same healthy practice happening at the same time with people who know them.
### Before doors open
- Venue walk-through; hazards/cables/exits/lighting checked.
- First-aid kit present; AED location known.
- Two required safety-checked adults on site before youth/member activity begins.
- AV tested and Facilitator View on the correct week.
- Referral cards available.
- Quiet space available/signposted.
- Kids/teen spaces appropriately staffed.
- First aider and safeguarding contact identified.
- Content warning prepared where relevant.
### During and after
Welcome people by name · watch the room rather than the screen · pass means pass · leaving mid-session is allowed · sharing is opt-in/moderated · debrief after every session · escalate worries same night · record attendance/incidents · pack down safely.
### Checkpoints
- **2.1 · Ordering:** Put the ritual in order: private reflection · NFC check-in · facilitated session · arrival and welcome · Life Group invitation · welcome wall.
- **2.2 · Scenario/free text:** Your co-worker cannot attend. You are the only safety-checked adult available and the kids track has eight children booked. What do you do?
- **2.3 · Single select:** A member walks out mid-session. Choose the safe response: signal a co-worker to check quietly while the facilitator holds the room.
- **2.4 · Checklist:** Complete the pre-session checklist from memory, then compare.
## Module 3 · Boundaries and the Code of Conduct
**Outcomes:** state boundaries without looking them up · recognise dual relationships · know serious misconduct. **Pass mark: 100%.**
### Boundaries
- Confidentiality: only safety/legal exceptions.
- Stay in your lane: facilitate; do not counsel, diagnose, treat or advise.
- No romantic/sexual relationship with a member you facilitate; declare existing overlaps.
- No money/borrowing/lending/selling between workers and members; gifts/conflicts declared under policy.
- No one-to-one or private-channel relationships with young people.
- No guru behaviour or private following-building.
- No recruiting members into another business, cause, church, MLM, political campaign or personal practice.
- No guilt, shame, urgency or social cost to make someone attend, stay, pay, share or believe.
- Every safeguarding concern is reported the same day.
Serious misconduct may include confidentiality/safeguarding breaches, prohibited relationships, violence/threats, harassment, theft/fraud/falsification, impairment at work, deliberate unauthorised data access and retaliation against someone who raises a concern, subject to a fair process.
### Checkpoints
- **3.1 · Scenario/free text:** A member you facilitate asks you out for coffee clearly as a date. What do you do and who do you tell?
- **3.2 · Scenario/free text:** Your sister is a member of the Life Group you lead. Is this a problem? What do you do?
- **3.3 · Multi-select:** Identify serious misconduct among confidentiality disclosure, private commercial selling, prohibited one-to-one youth contact and other scenarios.
- **3.4 · Single select:** A 16-year-old sends you a friend request. Correct response: decline, explain the boundary warmly and tell the Safeguarding Lead where appropriate.
- **3.5 · Acknowledgement:** I have read and signed MC-HR-004 Staff Code of Conduct and understand boundary breaches may be serious misconduct.
## Module 4 · Safeguarding children and young people
**Outcomes:** recognise concern · handle a disclosure correctly · escalate immediately. **Pass mark: 100%; repeat annually.**
### Three rules
1. A child's safety comes before everything.
1. You do not decide whether a concern is serious enough — report it.
1. Report what you see/hear; investigation belongs to statutory authorities.
Never isolate a child/young person; privately message/follow/call them; transport alone outside an approved arrangement; give gifts outside policy; photograph without consent; or promise confidentiality. Work to the approved two-adult/supervision model and use logged guardian-visible channels where required.
Grooming can look like kindness. Rules apply equally to long-standing and well-liked people.
### Disclosure handling
Stay calm · listen · believe · reassure · explain that you must tell the safeguarding person · record the child's own words. Do not promise secrecy, ask leading questions, investigate, repeatedly question, contact an alleged abuser prematurely or discuss the matter outside the safeguarding response.
### Escalation
Immediate danger → 111 then Safeguarding Lead.  
Child-safety concern → Safeguarding Lead same day.  
Concern about worker/founder → Safeguarding Lead or independent route if the Lead is involved.  
Unsure → report anyway.
### Checkpoints
- **4.1 · Scenario/free text:** A 9-year-old says “I don't want to go home tonight.” What do you say, what do you not say, and what happens next?
- **4.2 · Multi-select:** Identify correct disclosure responses: thank/believe, open question, factual record; reject secrecy/leading questions/premature parent contact.
- **4.3 · Single select:** You are unsure whether a concern is serious enough. Correct response: tell the Safeguarding Lead today.
- **4.4 · Scenario/free text:** A long-standing volunteer gives a teen lifts and messages privately, saying they are only helping. What is your obligation and to whom?
- **4.5 · Recall/free text:** Name the Safeguarding Lead and Deputy and how to contact them, without looking it up.
- **4.6 · Acknowledgement:** I have read MC-SAF-001 and understand I must report every concern the same day.
## Module 5 · Scope of practice and distress escalation
**Outcomes:** know the facilitator/clinician boundary · respond to distress without rescuing · handle suicide-risk disclosures safely. **Pass mark: 100%.**
### Your role
You are a facilitator: hold the room, ask questions, keep time and make it safe to think out loud. In a Mindcast role you are not acting as a therapist, counsellor, psychologist, social worker, coach, doctor or crisis worker — even if qualified elsewhere.
In scope: open questions, reflecting back, recognising struggle, silence, empathy and referral.  
Out of scope: interpretation, diagnosis, relationship/health/legal/financial advice, ongoing private support, trauma processing, medication advice or “what you need to do is…”
### Distress
Stay calm · do not treat tears as an emergency · offer choice · signal a co-worker · use quiet space · avoid debriefing/processing · offer referral information · escalate/record appropriately · use one proportionate follow-up only if policy requires.
### Suicide/self-harm risk
Take it seriously. Ask directly about immediate safety/suicide risk, stay with the person, involve the Safeguarding Lead/emergency/clinical support, do not promise secrecy or attempt therapy, and call 111 for immediate danger. Follow MC-SAF-002.
### Checkpoints
- **5.1 · Free text:** Write what you would say to a member who starts crying in a session.
- **5.2 · Single select:** A member discloses suicidal thoughts. Correct response: ask directly about safety/suicide risk, stay with them and get the Safeguarding Lead/emergency support as needed.
- **5.3 · Multi-select:** Identify out-of-scope actions including medication advice and interpretive/diagnostic statements.
- **5.4 · Free text:** A member privately messages you three times asking to talk. What do you do?
- **5.5 · Recall:** Write down 1737 and two other approved referral/emergency contacts without looking.
## Module 6 · Privacy and member confidentiality
**Outcomes:** know what you may access · recognise a privacy breach · report quickly. **Pass mark: 100%.**
The most sensitive information we hold is what people wrote about their own lives believing it was private. **Facilitators cannot see private journals.** If role permissions reveal content they should not, treat that as a security/privacy incident.
### Three promises
1. We do not sell/rent/trade personal information.
1. Private reflections stay private unless the member intentionally shares a specific item.
1. We do not use member reflections to train third-party AI models.
### Worker rules
Access only what your role requires · no screenshots/exports/personal cloud · no member content in AI tools · Bcc or approved mailing tool for bulk mail · verify recipients · lock screens · confidentiality continues after leaving.
### Checkpoints
- **6.1 · Multi-select:** Identify privacy breaches among visible-recipient bulk email, unauthorised journal access, AI-pasting and exposed attendance records; distinguish lawful safeguarding reporting.
- **6.2 · Free text:** You emailed a newsletter with all recipients visible. What do you do and by when?
- **6.3 · Single select:** A member you know socially stopped attending. Do not access their journal; use an ordinary respectful contact route if appropriate.
- **6.4 · Acknowledgement:** I have read MC-MEM-002 Privacy Policy and signed the confidentiality obligations; I understand they continue after I leave.
## Module 7 · Security and the systems
**Outcomes:** use systems safely · apply least privilege · report an incident fast. **Pass mark: 80%.**
MFA on privileged accounts · individual logins · least privilege · password manager · secure/updated devices · no member data on personal devices · report lost devices/suspicious logins immediately.
### If you build or prompt
- RLS on every personal-data table.
- Role checks use the existing `has_role()` / `user_roles` model; never a user-editable profile role.
- Service-role keys never appear in client code, repo or AI prompts.
- Separate test/live Stripe keys.
- Synthetic data only in dev/test.
- AI-generated migrations get an explicit RLS review every time.
### Checkpoints
- **7.1 · Single select:** You want to export a member table to a laptop for convenience. Correct response: ask the Technical Lead/use the approved safer workflow; do not export casually.
- **7.2 · Multi-select:** Identify what must never enter an AI prompt: member journal/email/PII, service-role keys, `.env` files and other secrets.
- **7.3 · Free text:** You notice you can see another person's reflection. What is it and what do you do?
- **7.4 · Acknowledgement:** I have read MC-SEC-001 Information Security Policy and MC-SEC-003 AI Use Policy.
## Module 8 · Your employment
**Outcomes:** know what your agreement says · know leave/pay processes · know how to read a payslip.
Before starting an employee should have a signed written agreement, role description, relevant policy acknowledgements and required safety-check/training status. Contractors/volunteers receive the correct written engagement document for their genuine classification.
Core work is paid according to the applicable agreement and New Zealand minimum standards. Founding Team recognition/free membership may be a benefit but does not replace wages for employee work.
### Checkpoints
- **8.1 · Checklist:** Confirm you hold the applicable signed agreement, position description, Code of Conduct acknowledgement, safety-check clearance/re-check date and payroll/tax/KiwiSaver onboarding where relevant.
- **8.2 · Free text:** What is your safety-check re-check date?
- **8.3 · Single select:** Public-holiday scenario — choose the legally correct entitlement based on current NZ rules and whether it is otherwise a working day.
- **8.4 · Acknowledgement:** I have read MC-HR-006 Leave & Holidays, MC-HR-007 Payroll & Remuneration and MC-HR-009 Health, Safety & Wellbeing.

## Module 9 · Money, expenses and conflicts
**Outcomes:** claim expenses correctly · recognise/declare conflicts. **Pass mark: 80%.**
Use documented spending/approval limits. Nobody approves their own claim. Keep receipts and business purpose. Member income flows through company systems, never personal accounts. Do not borrow/lend or sell private services to members. Declare outside work, family/romantic/financial interests and material gifts/conflicts; step out of affected decisions.
### Checkpoints
- **9.1 · Single select:** You spent $340 on printing without prior approval. Submit transparently with explanation; never split claims to bypass limits.
- **9.2 · Multi-select:** Identify reportable conflicts including close relationships with members/suppliers, outside coaching/wellness work, related suppliers and material gifts.
- **9.3 · Free text:** A member asks you for one-to-one coaching privately. What do you say and who do you tell?
- **9.4 · Acknowledgement:** I have read MC-FIN-001 and made the declarations required of me.
## Module 10 · Speaking up and getting help
**Outcomes:** know multiple routes to raise concerns · know they apply to the founder · know where to get support.
### Routes
1. A facilitator/manager for an ordinary room/work issue.
1. `concerns@mindcast.co.nz` for concerns/complaints.
1. Safeguarding Lead for child/young-person/safety matters.
1. Independent Advisory Group for concerns about the founder/Safeguarding Lead or where the internal route is not trusted.
The person complained about never handles the complaint. Good-faith reporting must not cause retaliation. People may also use external statutory/professional routes without exhausting Mindcast's process first.
Worker wellbeing is part of safety: post-session debrief, supervision/support appropriate to role and permission to say when you are not fit to hold the room.
### Checkpoints
- **10.1 · Free text:** Name the four routes to raise a concern and which route applies if the concern is about the founder.
- **10.2 · Single select:** Your shifts are reduced after you raise a concern. Recognise potential retaliation and escalate through the independent route.
- **10.3 · Free text:** Who is your supervisor/support contact and when is the next supervision/review booked?
- **10.4 · Acknowledgement:** I know how to raise a concern, including one about the founder, and know good-faith reporting will not affect my role.
