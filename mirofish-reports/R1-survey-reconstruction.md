# R1 Survey Reconstruction — What the Agents' Answers Would Have Been

**Simulation:** `sim_bacb50373131` (Taupō launch, two weeks, 21 agents, 109+ rounds, ~700 actions)
**Method:** The interview environment was dead, so these answers are reconstructed from each agent's *observed behaviour* — every post and comment they actually made, quoted verbatim from `actions.jsonl`. Where an agent left no signal on a question, it is marked **—**.

**Three honesty flags before you read:**
1. **Seeded repetition.** Most agents' round-0 posts were re-broadcast at r9 on both platforms. Unique positions are fewer than raw counts suggest.
2. **Persona drift.** Several agents argued against their own label: `members` raised the grooming accusation, `children` ran a #cultwatch critique, `Founders` made the MLM accusation. Treat agent *names* as labels, not identities.
3. **Truncation.** Long posts are quoted to ~300 chars where the log was trimmed during extraction.

## THE CRITICS

### observers (sociologist — the most substantive critic, 11 posts)
1. **What is Mindcast:** A for-profit personal-development organisation redeploying religious organisational forms — "textbook organizational forms from religious tradition, redeployed for a secular self-improvement framework."
2. **Harm likelihood:** High (~7–8/10). Not alarmist but structural: "small group assignment is a powerful mechanism" (r96); the leader→facilitator rename is "a well-documented linguistic decoupling" (r92).
3. **Feeling:** Watchful suspicion.
4. **Charter first impression:** "Why would a for-profit company need a charter? It's a marketing ploy to make them look legitimate. If you have to publish a document saying you're not a cult, you're probably a cult." (r0)
5. **Most/least believed:** Most: "the commitments around no urgency, no scarcity, and no retention calls are genuinely more robust than what most organizations — for-profit or otherwise — voluntarily publish" (r48). Least: "transparency is our promise… repeated far more often than the transparency itself is demonstrated" — "performed transparency" (r47).
6. **Does denying cult increase suspicion:** Yes — the circular logic in Q4 is their signature move.
7. **What would make them believe:** Independent, outward verification. They explicitly credited Mindcast's promise to "publish the full curriculum framework and… explore a community advisory panel with independent educators and parents" as "exactly the kind of verifiable, outward…" step (r46).
8. **Child attendance:** No — private homes are "unsupervised spaces where power dynamics can develop outside institutional visibility" (r19).
10. **Is vetting enough:** "police vetting is a necessary but insufficient safeguard" (r19).
17. **Founder/anti-guru:** The facilitator model is the problem — terminology change without power change (r92).
22. **Disclaimers helped or hurt:** Hurt — "repeated defensive declarations ('not a church, not a cult, not therapy')" read as signalling the opposite (r45).
24. **Did view change:** No — hardened. By r93: "Another Sunday, another coordinated response… Five separate accounts, all echoing the same talking points."
25. **What would move them:** Enforcement evidence — complaints procedure actually used, financials actually published, dissent actually tolerated.
26. **Recommend:** Warn with methodology: observe behaviour over time, verify each written claim.

## WHAT THIS ACTUALLY TELLS YOU (vs. the report's claims)

1. **The report claimed nobody changed position. Wrong.** Two documented arcs: `young people` (cult accusation → youth-program defence) and `local community Facebook groups` ("skeptical at first" → attended free sessions). Both converted through **direct observation/attendance**, not argument. Your real-world lever is the free session, not the charter.
2. **The report claimed no skeptic found the charter credible. Wrong.** `observers` — the harshest critic — called the no-urgency/no-scarcity/no-retention commitments "genuinely more robust than what most organizations voluntarily publish." The charter's *operational* clauses landed; its *existential* framing ("not a cult") backfired.
3. **The church-resemblance frame was contested from inside the simulation**: churches themselves distinguished foundation from structure, and yoga studios distinguished form from purpose. The frame is sticky but not unanimous.
4. **The Life Groups in private homes are the single most emotionally charged vulnerability** ("That's how grooming starts"). The only agent that defused it did so with *precedent* (churches: we've done home groups for years) — consider borrowing that normalisation rather than only listing safeguards.
5. **Price comparisons cut both ways**: gyms say $29 is steep vs. physical results; teens say it's cheap vs. $80–120 counselling. The therapy comparison is your friend but counselling services will police it — anchor value against *coaching/counselling*, never against *therapy*.
6. **Founder tension is real and was the first critique posted** (r0, two agents independently: portrait+quote contradicts "no authority"; "classic MLM territory"). The org's rebuttal (identification, not authority) was repeated verbatim so often it became evidence for the "coordinated response" critique. Fewer, more human founder answers would serve better.
